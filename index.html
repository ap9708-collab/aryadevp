Biography & Mathematical Trajectory

Aryadev Padhi

As a graduate student at the Courant Institute, I am involved in several research projects. No doubt, the most exciting one is my Master's thesis, advised by Prof. Esteban Tabak, on Geometric Flows and Schwarzschild Geometry: A Study of Curvature, Singularities, and Evolution in General Relativity. My work investigates the effect of curvature and singularity development on geometric flows in curved Riemannian manifolds, focusing specifically on Schwarzschild-type geometries and their connection to Ricci curvature and black hole geometry. The analytical and computational aspects involve techniques from differential geometry, functional analysis, nonlinear PDEs, and level-set methods, which reflect my primary research interests.

I am also part of an NSF-funded research group with Kwesi Forson and Dr. Jennifer Güler at the University of Virginia's Güler Malaria Lab. Our aim is to develop a quantitative framework for understanding how Plasmodium falciparum parasites make cell-fate decisions between normal development and latency under nutrient stress and antimalarial drug exposure. My work involves developing mathematical models to investigate drug effectiveness, saturation points, and the conditions under which outbreaks could occur, connecting mathematical predictions with experimental observations.

I have also worked on the ecological side of mathematical biology, particularly nonlinear dynamical systems, pattern formation, and computational differential equations. I have a published paper in the Electronic Journal of Qualitative Theory of Differential Equations, alongside preprints on traveling-wave solutions in Lotka–Volterra systems, group defense mechanisms, and Turing patterns in cross-diffusive systems. Complementing my analytical work, I co-developed a Physics-Informed Neural Network framework for solving differential equations with periodic coefficients. I am also working on global boundedness and codimension-2 Turing–Hopf bifurcation in a three-species Hastings–Powell system with cross-diffusion, where I am investigating the analytical challenges involving diffusion matrices, center manifold reduction, and weak solution regularity.

Here are my favorite theorems and math things that I appreciate that they exist.

01 · Compactness

Rellich–Kondrachov

Boundedness alone sometimes wouldn't imply convergence; however, if provided enough regularity, one can extract convergence in a weaker norm.

02 · Point-Set Topology

Hausdorff Spaces

You can separate any two distinct points by putting each in its own non-overlapping open neighborhood.

03 · Inequality

Young's Inequality

For any two non-negative real numbers $a, b \ge 0$ and conjugate exponents $p, q > 1$ with $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$: $$ab \le \frac{a^p}{p} + \frac{b^q}{q}$$

04 · Measure Theory

Radon–Nikodym Theorem

If you have two ways of measuring the size of regions in a space with an initial measure ($\mu$) and a new measure ($\nu$), and the new measure never assigns weight to anything the initial says has zero size, then you can calculate the new measure simply by applying a reweighting function to the initial measure.

05 · Spectral Theory

Spectral Theorem

any symmetric or normal linear operator can be broken down into a set of mutually perpendicular directions along which the operator acts purely as simple stretching or scaling..
